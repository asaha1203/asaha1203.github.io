Hey! I'm Amit Saha, a fourth-year undergrad at Georgia Tech studying computer science and mathematics. I'm fortunate to work with Pan Li, Juba Ziani, Jacob Abernethy, and Salil Vadhan (via OpenDP).

My research focuses on the theoretical foundations of responsible learning. Specifically, I focus on formalizing societal goals through {notions of trustworthiness}, understanding their {statistical, computational} barriers, and identifying minimal assumptions which enable {accurate, efficient} algorithms. I seek to turn these insights into problem-agnostic principles for trustworthy algorithm design.

More broadly, I am interested in the complexity of learning and decision-making.

I will be applying to Ph.D. programs for admission in Fall 2027.

Currently

Recently, I've been thinking about (1) private in-context learning, (2) information-computation gaps in private statistical estimation under privacy and sparsity, and (3) computational rates for query-efficient private and replicable learning.

I've also been learning about the Sum-of-Squares hierarchy and its applications to the design of algorithms for statistical inference.
